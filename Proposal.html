Tentative Project Title

ClimaScope: Forecasting the Empire Skies

Motivation

Climate variability and extreme weather increasingly affect New York State. Understanding local patterns is vital for public health, agriculture, and infrastructure. Using five years of weather data, this project visualizes and models temperature, precipitation, and wind speed to reveal key climatic drivers and support regional climate planning.

Intended Final Products

Interactive Visualizations showing spatial and temporal patterns of temperature, precipitation, and wind speed across New York counties from 2016–2020.
Cleaned and Integrated Dataset with detailed data dictionary and quality summary.
Modeling and Forecasting Results including unsupervised climate clustering and statewide predictive models for daily maximum temperature (seasonal ARIMAX with Fourier terms and XGBoost), together with model comparison.
Final Report and GitHub Repository containing reproducible code, documentation, and all figures for transparent analysis.

Anticipated Data Sources

The meteorology datasets were downloaded from “Daily meteorological Gridmet variables by United States administrative boundaries”, Harvard Dataverse. To monitor the changing patterns in the past seasons and years, meteorological data from 2016 to 2020 on the county level.

Corresponding county information was downloaded from tiger/line Shapefiles. Due to the lapse in federal funding, portions of the United States Census Bureau were unavailable. tl_2020_state and tl_2024_county were downloaded to obtain additional New York state and county information to facilitate further visualizations and analysis.

Planned Analyses and Visualizations

Exploratory Data Analysis: Visualize spatial and temporal patterns of temperature, precipitation, and wind speed using histograms, density plots, heatmaps, and county-level maps. Examine correlations and temporal dependencies among key variables.

Time-Series and Predictive Modeling: Use daily statewide-averaged meteorological series to fit seasonal ARIMAX models with Fourier terms and XGBoost models for forecasting maximum temperature. Compare their performance using a common train–test split, residual diagnostics, and accuracy metrics, and discuss trade-offs between interpretability and predictive flexibility.

Visualization Deliverables: Deliver interactive and static visualizations, including maps, seasonal plots, and correlation displays, summarizing key findings clearly and reproducibly.

Unsupervised Climate Clustering and PCA: Use k-means clustering to identify distinct county-level climate types, selecting k=2 via elbow and silhouette diagnostics. Apply PCA to visualize cluster separation in a low-dimensional space and combine boxplots and county maps to summarize and interpret the resulting climate regimes.

Coding Challenges

Key challenges include integrating large meteorological datasets, managing spatial data for county-level mapping, and aligning geographic identifiers. Implementing time-series models (ARIMAX + Fourier) and XGBoost efficiently in R and ensuring reproducibility through clear, well-documented code will be essential.
